## 1. Introduction

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) are among the most prevalent neurodevelopmental conditions worldwide, each affecting approximately 1-2% and 5-7% of the global population, respectively. Clinically, the two disorders are frequently comorbid: epidemiological studies estimate that 50-70% of individuals with ASD also meet diagnostic criteria for ADHD, a co-occurrence increasingly recognized in the neurodivergent community as "AuDHD" [CITE: Vanderbilt AuDHD review; APA blog]. Despite this striking clinical overlap, the molecular basis of ASD-ADHD comorbidity remains poorly understood, representing a fundamental gap in neurodevelopmental biology.

Efforts to characterize the molecular relationship between ASD and ADHD at the gene level have yielded surprisingly limited overlap. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) and differential expression analyses have identified largely distinct gene sets for each disorder, with some shared genetic liability at the population level but minimal convergence at the level of individual risk genes or differentially expressed transcripts [CITE: PMC11481320 - Characterizing genetic pathways unique to ASD]. This discordance between high clinical co-occurrence and low gene-level overlap represents a fundamental paradox that conventional gene-centric approaches have been unable to resolve. We propose that this paradox arises because the shared biology underlying ASD-ADHD comorbidity is not encoded in individual genes, but rather in the organization of gene regulatory networks — a level of biological complexity that requires systems-level analytical frameworks to detect.

Gene co-expression network analysis provides precisely this systems-level perspective. Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) identifies groups of genes — modules — whose expression is tightly coordinated across samples, reflecting shared regulatory inputs, biological pathways, or cellular processes [CITE: Langfelder & Horvath, 2008, BMC Bioinformatics]. Applied to ASD postmortem brain tissue, WGCNA has demonstrated that transcriptional dysregulation in ASD is convergent across genetically heterogeneous cases, implicating neuronal splicing and immune-glial modules as core pathophysiological features [CITE: Voineagu et al., 2011, Nature]. More recently, WGCNA applied to ASD blood transcriptomes has revealed NK-cell signaling modules with replicated hub genes, suggesting that co-expression network architecture captures disease-relevant biology even in peripheral tissue [CITE: Horiuchi et al., 2021; ASD Transcriptomics review preprint]. For ADHD, WGCNA applied to peripheral blood mononuclear cells from 270 ADHD cases identified co-expression modules associated with ADHD status, revealing immune-metabolic dysregulation at the network level [CITE: Translational Psychiatry ADHD WGCNA 2022]. However, no study has applied a WGCNA-based framework to jointly interrogate ASD and ADHD transcriptomes with the explicit goal of defining shared versus disorder-specific network architecture — a necessary prerequisite for computationally operationalizing AuDHD biology.

An important complementary approach to co-expression network analysis is protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis, which maps the physical interactions between protein products of co-expressed genes. While co-expression networks capture transcriptional coordination, PPI networks capture structural and functional protein relationships — two distinct and complementary layers of biological organization. The convergence of co-expression hub gene identity with PPI centrality provides particularly strong evidence for biologically coherent regulatory modules, as it implies that transcriptionally coordinated genes also physically interact, forming druggable protein complexes. This WGCNA-PPI integration framework has been applied in cancer and metabolic diseases [CITE: Nangraj et al. 2020 Frontiers Pharmacology], but has not previously been used in the context of neurodevelopmental comorbidity to distinguish transcriptional from structural co-regulation.

In this study, we construct a weighted gene co-expression network from merged whole blood transcriptome data from ASD and typically developing control samples (GSE111175 + GSE42133, n=238), identify the primary ASD-associated co-expression module (ME11), and validate hub genes from this module against the STRING protein-protein interaction database to test centrality convergence. We further cross-validate ME11 dysregulation in an independent ADHD whole blood transcriptome dataset (GSE159104, n=76) to determine whether ME11 represents shared AuDHD biology or ASD-specific molecular dysregulation. Finally, we characterize the biological processes underlying ME11 through Reactome pathway enrichment analysis. Together, this integrated WGCNA-PPI framework provides the first network-topological characterization of AuDHD-relevant molecular architecture, demonstrates the utility of this approach for distinguishing disorder-specific from shared biology, and identifies a spliceosomal co-expression module as a candidate ASD-specific molecular signature in whole blood transcriptomes.
